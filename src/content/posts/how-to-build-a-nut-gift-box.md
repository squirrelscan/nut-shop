A good nut gift box is easy to like and easy to use. It does not need ribbons the size of a dinner plate. It needs a thoughtful mix, a sensible size, and a few things the recipient can eat now and a few they can keep. Here is how to plan one, whether you are filling a tin for a colleague or a hamper for the whole family.

## Start with the person

Before choosing a single nut, think about who it is for.

- **The snacker** wants roasted, salted and flavoured nuts they can open and eat.
- **The baker** wants ingredients: [ground almonds](/products/ground-almonds), chopped walnuts, pecan pieces.
- **The toast-and-spread fan** wants jars and butters.
- **The host** wants something that looks good on a table and can be shared.

If you are not sure, aim for a mix of snackable and cookable. It is hard to get that wrong.

## Aim for contrast

A box that is all one texture or flavour gets dull quickly. Include variety:

- **Something crunchy and roasted**, such as [dry-roasted almonds](/products/dry-roasted-almonds) or salted cashews.
- **Something sweet**, like [maple-glazed pecans](/products/maple-glazed-pecans) or honey-roasted peanuts.
- **Something with a kick**, like a chilli or smoked flavour.
- **Something rich and spreadable**, such as a jar of nut butter.
- **Something plain**, for those who prefer simple flavours or want to cook.

Our post on [building a trail mix](/blog/a-guide-to-building-a-trail-mix) takes the same approach on a smaller scale: balance sweet, salty, crunchy and chewy, and you rarely go wrong.

## Choose a theme

Themes make a box feel intentional.

### The tasting box

Several small portions of different nuts, so the recipient can compare. Almonds, cashews, hazelnuts and pecans, each in a separate bag, plus a card saying which is which. It is a natural fit for curious eaters.

### The baker's box

Raw and chopped nuts, some flour-style ingredients, a recipe card. You could include a handwritten copy of a favourite, such as our [walnut banana bread](/blog/walnut-banana-bread-recipe).

### The breakfast box

A jar of granola or crunch mix, a pot of nut butter, a few single-serve bags of nuts for the desk. Easy to use, and it feels like a treat.

### The festive box

Spiced, glazed and sweet nuts, plus something rich and chocolatey. This is the one where it is fine to go a little extravagant.

## Think about size and packing

- **Small and neat** suits a tin, a jar or a small box with a few items.
- **Medium** suits a shallow tray with four to six items.
- **Large** is for a hamper with jars, bags and perhaps a bowl or board.

Pack heavy jars on the bottom and lighter bags on top. Keep sweet and savoury items in their own bags so flavours do not mix. Fill any gaps with paper or a folded cloth so nothing rattles. If you are posting it, add a layer of padding, and check the [shipping and returns](/shipping-and-returns) page for timing so it arrives fresh.

## Keep freshness in mind

Nuts stay best when they are cool, dry and sealed. Avoid packing them next to anything that gives off heat or moisture. Our post on [storing nuts so they stay fresh](/blog/storing-nuts-so-they-stay-fresh) has more tips you can pass on to the recipient. A small card with a "best enjoyed within" note is a thoughtful touch.

## A note on allergies

Always think about who will open the box. If you do not know a person's allergies, ask, or choose something other than nuts. If you are gifting to a household with allergy concerns, include labels or a list of contents, and tell them what is inside. Read the label on every product. For anything medical, a qualified professional is the right person to ask. The [FSA allergy guidance](https://www.food.gov.uk/safety-hygiene/food-allergy-and-intolerance) and [FDA food allergy page](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-labeling-nutrition/food-allergies) are useful general references.

Add a short handwritten note and a suggestion of how to enjoy it. A gift is never really about the nuts. They are just a very tasty way of saying you thought of someone.
