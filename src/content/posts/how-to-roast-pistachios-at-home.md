Pistachios are one of the easiest nuts to roast, and one of the easiest to overdo. They are small, they are full of oil, and they turn from fragrant to bitter faster than almonds or hazelnuts. A short, gentle roast is the secret. Here is how to get a crisp, savoury pistachio that keeps its bright green colour.

## Start with shelled, unsalted nuts

If you can, begin with raw, shelled pistachios without salt. Salted nuts will taste fine after a roast, but you lose control of the seasoning, and the surface salt can scorch. Raw kernels are pale green and slightly soft. After roasting they become firmer, a little darker and much more aromatic.

Spread the nuts on a tray in a single layer. Crowding them means the ones in the middle steam instead of roast, so use two trays if you have a lot.

## Roasting in the oven

Heat the oven to 150 degrees Celsius. This is lower than you would use for almonds, and the lower temperature is what protects the colour. Roast for eight to twelve minutes, shaking the tray halfway through.

You are listening and smelling as much as looking. When the kitchen smells warm and nutty and the nuts feel dry rather than soft, take the tray out. They will keep cooking on the hot metal, so tip them onto a cool plate straight away.

If you are not sure, take one out and let it cool for a minute. Bite it. A good roasted pistachio snaps cleanly and tastes sweet, not grassy.

## Roasting in a dry pan

For a small handful, a frying pan is quicker. Use a heavy pan with no oil over a medium-low heat. Keep the nuts moving with a wooden spoon for four to six minutes. The danger here is hot spots, so do not walk away.

The pan method is good when you need warm nuts for a salad, because you can toss them straight in. It is less even than the oven, so it suits small batches.

## When to add salt

Salt sticks best to warm nuts. While they are still hot, toss them with a pinch of fine sea salt in a bowl. A teaspoon of water mixed with the salt first helps it cling if you like a stronger coating, but for most tastes a light dusting is enough.

If you want a spiced version, add the spices at the same time as the salt. Ground cumin and a little smoked paprika are a good pairing. Sweet versions work too: a teaspoon of honey stirred into the hot nuts, then returned to the oven for two minutes.

## Keeping the colour

Pistachios are prized for their green. Heat, light and time all fade it. To keep it, roast at a low temperature, cool the nuts quickly and store them in a closed jar away from the window. The nuts will taste good long after the colour dulls, but fresh green nuts look much better on a plate.

## Ways to use roasted pistachios

Chop them and scatter them over yoghurt or porridge. Fold them into a salad of grains, herbs and lemon. Pulse them into a crumb for fish or chicken. Press them into the top of a loaf cake or a tray of baked oats. ## Storing roasted nuts

Let the nuts cool completely before they go into a jar, because trapped warmth makes them soft. Keep the jar somewhere cool and dark, and eat the nuts within a few weeks if you roast them yourself. ## Quick reference

Oven: 150 degrees Celsius, eight to twelve minutes, shake halfway. Pan: medium-low heat, four to six minutes, keep stirring. Salt while warm. Cool on a plate, then jar. That is all there is to it, and a tray of home-roasted pistachios will be gone faster than you expect.
