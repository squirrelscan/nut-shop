A good trail mix is a small act of engineering. It should be crunchy and chewy in turns, a little sweet and a little salty, and sturdy enough to survive a rucksack. A bad one is a bag of raisins with a few sad peanuts hiding at the bottom. The difference is mostly down to balance, and balance is easy once you know the rules of thumb.

## Start with a ratio

A reliable starting point is the 2:1:1 rule, measured by volume.

- **Two parts nuts and seeds:** the backbone of the mix
- **One part dried fruit:** for sweetness and chew
- **One part extras:** chocolate, coconut, crunchy cereal or something savoury

For a mix that fills one large jar, that might be two cups of nuts, a cup of fruit and a cup of extras. Scale it up or down as you like. If you prefer something more savoury, shrink the fruit and extras to a half part each.

## Choose your nuts

Nuts do the heavy lifting, so give them some thought. Variety in size, shape and texture is what makes handfuls interesting.

- **Almonds:** firm, reliable and a little sweet. [Raw almonds](/products/raw-almonds) work, and roasted ones add depth.
- **Cashews:** buttery and soft-crunchy. [Raw cashews](/products/raw-cashews) bring a gentle sweetness that suits fruit.
- **Peanuts:** technically a legume, but they behave like a nut and keep the budget friendly.
- **Walnuts and pecans:** richer and slightly bitter, so use them in smaller amounts.
- **Seeds:** pumpkin and sunflower seeds add crunch and colour without competing for attention.

Aim for at least three kinds. Two nuts and a seed is a perfectly good start.

## Add dried fruit with care

Fruit adds sweetness and a chewy contrast, but too much turns the mix sticky and heavy.

- Raisins and sultanas are the traditional choice and almost impossible to get wrong.
- Chopped dates or apricots add a deeper, jammier sweetness. Cut them into pea-sized pieces so they spread through the mix.
- Dried cranberries or cherries give a tart note.
- Banana chips and mango strips suit a tropical mix.

Large pieces of fruit can clump together, so chop them or toss them in a spoonful of the other ingredients before mixing.

## Pick your extras

The extras are where you give the mix its personality.

- **Chocolate:** chips or small chunks. In warm weather they melt, so use dark chocolate or plan to eat the mix in a cool place.
- **Coconut:** toasted flakes add lightness.
- **Crunchy cereal or pretzels:** these add salt and a different kind of crunch.
- **Spices:** a pinch of cinnamon or chilli on the nuts changes the whole character.

## Balance sweet, salty and crunchy

Taste is a three-way tug of war. A few tips:

1. **Salt is your friend.** A mix with no salt tastes flat. Use salted nuts, or toss the nuts with a pinch while warm.
2. **Pair sweet with savoury.** Chocolate and salted almonds, or dried mango and chilli cashews, are classic contrasts.
3. **Mix textures.** If every ingredient is hard, you are chewing a bag of gravel. Add something soft, something crisp, something chewy.
4. **Keep pieces roughly the same size.** Otherwise the heavy bits sink to the bottom and you end up with a layer of raisins.

### Three starting recipes

**The everyday mix:** 2 cups almonds, 1 cup raisins, 1/2 cup dark chocolate chips, 1/2 cup pumpkin seeds.

**The tropical mix:** 1 cup cashews, 1 cup banana chips, 1/2 cup toasted coconut, 1/2 cup chopped dried mango, 1/2 cup macadamia or peanuts.

**The savoury kick:** 2 cups mixed roasted nuts, 1 cup pretzel pieces, 1/2 cup sunflower seeds, 1 teaspoon smoked paprika and a pinch of salt.

## Toast first for more flavour

A few minutes in the oven wakes up the nuts. Spread them on a tray and roast at 160C (320F) for 8 to 12 minutes, stirring once. Cool them completely before mixing them with fruit or chocolate. Our guide on [how to roast almonds at home](/blog/how-to-roast-almonds-at-home) gives the details.

## Keep it crunchy

Moisture is the enemy. Dried fruit holds water and nuts absorb it, so a mix stored for a long time can turn soft and slightly chewy. To slow that:

- Use an airtight container, and fill it as full as you can so there is little air.
- Keep it out of heat and sunlight.
- Make smaller batches more often.
- For long walks, carry the mix in a sealed bag rather than loose in a pocket.

Chocolate and fruit also affect keeping times, which is another reason to make small batches. For more on how nuts hold up in storage, see [storing nuts so they stay fresh](/blog/storing-nuts-so-they-stay-fresh).

## A note on allergies

Mixes combine many ingredients, so anyone with an allergy should read the label on every component. If you are making a mix to share, tell people exactly what is in it. For general guidance, see the [FDA page on food allergies](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-labeling-nutrition/food-allergies).

## A last word

Pick three nuts, one fruit, one extra and a pinch of salt. Taste, adjust, and make a note of what worked. Within a couple of batches you will have a signature mix that no shop-bought bag quite matches, and a few good reasons to go for a walk.
